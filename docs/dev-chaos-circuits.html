Chaos Circuits

From the hovercraft archive to new routes

November 20–21, 2026

The recovered project line, two stunt courses, and the gap between route validation and a race.

Preserve the starting point

The Chaos Circuits workspace retains the historical archives and builds a separate active source and design tree. The modern work draws on Assault Knights hover and roadway ideas, with a Q26 game-code branch for hover movement. The recovered models and later prototype craft give the team assets for physics and collision experiments.

Author two distinct terrain courses

Tidal Braid follows the shoreline through a buried corkscrew and a 65° loop. Glacier Switch uses a raised route and a −40° loop. Each course has ten staggered starts, eight ordered checkpoints, a repair and recharge pit and boost approaches before the corkscrew, jump and loop.

Check the geometry and the paths

The latest candidate added low bevelled roll rails with tapered ends and retained a 432-unit clear centre lane. The source validation checked both route graphs and both workbench views per course. The alternate lanes are directed from split to merge. Their source path checks establish graph connectivity. Vehicle physics and AI route selection run through different systems.

Keep navigation open

The wide-bound AAS probes for Tidal reached geometry processing without writing a completed navigation file in the bounded runs. A final navigation build, alternate-lane bot behaviour, collision coverage and a real race remain milestones for both course candidates.
