Preserving Aurora Canyon through conversion

October 20, 2026

The 10 km authored core, native terrain tiles and why the conversion uses a wider envelope.

Keep authored coordinates

Aurora Canyon’s authored world is 10 km square. Its retained route graph has 38 nodes, 47 edges and 10 independent cycles. Four fork families fan out into three lanes at different elevations and head back to a shared merge. Authoring separates the heightfield from the road network so bridges, ramps, tunnels and gaps can have their own geometry.

Prepare a conversion and check the terrain

The Q26 conversion preserved that 10 km core at the original scale. The terrain parser rounds the native boundary through a radial falloff, so a strict 10 km runtime envelope affected the corners. A 14.2 km guard envelope protects the original area without rescaling it. A 16-tile native terrain check passed 8,321 height queries, with a maximum measured error of about 2.51 cm.

Keep source and conversion evidence distinct

The conversion’s extended review graph has 57 nodes, 67 edges and 11 checked crossings. Those additions belong to the conversion review. The hub map shows the original authored graph. Road meshes, structural support clearance and the terrain sample checks each cover a different part of the conversion.

Finish the vehicle path

A playable conversion needs the complete road and tunnel collision scene, native terrain-hole support and driving checks. Full editor integration and loaded-editor frame rate are also separate gates. The showcase illustrates the design without carrying the original SDK or game assets.
